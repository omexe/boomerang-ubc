# Attacks, one by one

Each row is a way someone might try to take money they are not owed, or stop others getting theirs, with the rule that answers it, what the rule does not cover, and the test that shows it. Tests are in `contracts/test/attacks.test.ts` unless another file is named. Rows marked "not defended" are stated limits, not bugs.

| # | Attack | Answer in the contract | What remains | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Outsiders pledge early only to farm the bonus | The bonus is `pledgedEarly x min(pool, rate x weighted) / target`: never more than the pool, and small when few pledge early | Up to the bonus paid, when nobody completes the campaign in time | `A1 the bonus never exceeds the pool or the rate` |
| A2 | Late pledges, or a rush in the last second, pump the bonus | Nothing after the early deadline changes `pledgedEarly` or `weighted` | none | `A2 pledges after the early deadline leave the bonus unchanged` |
| A3 | The recipient backs its own campaign through other wallets | not defended: the contract cannot see who owns a wallet. The sponsor chooses the recipient; the pool caps the loss | Up to the pool on a miss | none |
| A4 | The sponsor's insiders back it from private wallets | not defended, same reason | Up to the pool | none |
| A5 | One campaign's payouts draw on another campaign's money | Every amount owed is computed from its own campaign's totals; paying with another campaign's id pays nothing | none | `A5 campaigns are isolated` |
| A6 | Waiting to the last moment of the early window for full reward | Linear weight falls to zero at the early deadline | Flat weight, if the sponsor chose it, pays the same throughout the window. With an early window longer than a million seconds (about 11.5 days), the last seconds round to zero weight but still count as early | `A6 a later early pledge earns less under linear weight` |
| A7 | A pledge landing exactly on a deadline second | The early-deadline second earns no weight; the closing second is refused | none | `pledge.test.ts`: `boundaries` |
| A8 | The sponsor tops up a little to move the bonus or fake demand | The sponsor's account cannot pledge; `rescue` takes exactly the whole gap or nothing | The sponsor's other wallets are ordinary backers (A4) | `A8 the sponsor cannot top up in part` |
| A9 | Pledge, then pull out before the deadline | There is no withdrawal; `pay` moves nothing while a campaign is open | none | `A9 no way out before the outcome` |
| A10 | The sponsor wash-pledges, as payer or as named backer | Refused in both roles | Other wallets (A4) | `A10 sponsor as payer or backer is refused` |
| A11 | A different token, a direct transfer, or a claim of authority | One token, fixed at deployment; only `fundMatch`, `fundBonus`, `pledge`, `pledgeFor` and `rescue` create anything owed; the sponsor and the funder are known by sender | A direct transfer to the contract is a gift nobody can claim | `A11 a direct transfer creates nothing owed` |
| A12 | A payout that cannot be delivered blocks everyone | A failed payout stays owed and is skipped in a batch; the backer can redirect | A recipient or sponsor that cannot receive USDC keeps its own side unpaid | `pay.test.ts`: `pay.blocked` |
| A13 | Thousands of dust pledges to make closing a campaign too expensive | A minimum pledge; `finalize` does the same work whatever the number of backers | Paying many backers costs gas per backer, paid by whoever calls | `A13 finalize costs the same with 2 backers or 40` |
| A14 | A final gap smaller than the minimum locks the campaign short | A pledge below the minimum is accepted when it closes the gap | none | `pledge.test.ts`: `a gap smaller than the minimum` |
| A15 | Rounding, or a campaign with no early pledges | Every division rounds down; with no early weight the bonus is zero and the pool returns whole | At most one base unit of dust per bonus payout stays in the contract for good | `pay.test.ts`: `only rounding dust`; `outcomes.test.ts`: `nobody early` |
| A16 | The sponsor opens a campaign without locking the pots, or changes terms later | Pledging is refused unless both pots were in before opening; no function changes terms | none | `A16 no function can change a campaign's terms`; `outcomes.test.ts`: `void` |
| A17 | The sponsor disappears | No outcome and no payout needs the sponsor | A rescue needs the sponsor, and none is promised | `A17 everything settles without the sponsor` |
| A18 | A token that calls back into the contract during a transfer | Every state-changing function is guarded against re-entry. On the way out, a payout is marked paid and taken off the amount owed before tokens move, and put back if the token refuses. On the way in, the pledge is recorded first and the amount owed rises only after the money is seen to arrive | none with real USDC, which makes no callbacks | `A18 a re-entering token is refused`; `tokens.test.ts`: `a token calling back into any of the eleven is refused with Reentered` |
| A19 | Getting paid twice | A backer's record is marked paid before tokens move; a repeated name in a batch pays once | none | `A19 a backer is paid once` |
| A20 | Calling `pay` for someone else to redirect their money | Paying is open to anyone and only ever pays the recorded destination; only the backer can change it | none | `A20 the caller of pay gains nothing` |
| A21 | Amounts at the limits overflow the arithmetic | Target, match and pool are capped at 10^15; every product fits | none | `A21 the largest allowed campaign settles exactly` |
| A22 | Naming the contract itself as recipient, backer or payout destination, so a payout goes nowhere | Refused in all three places | none | `edges.test.ts`: `the contract itself is never a recipient` |
| A23 | A token that moves money but answers "no", or answers "yes" without moving it, on the way out | not defended. Incoming money is checked against the balance; outgoing money is taken at the token's word. The contract is fixed to USDC at deployment, which reverts when a transfer fails | A dishonest token could break the books. None is ever accepted: the token cannot be changed | `tokens.test.ts` covers the honest failures: answering no, answering nothing, keeping a fee, a malformed reply |

## The cross-chain router

Tests are in `contracts/test/router.test.ts`. The router has run only against a stand-in for Circle's transmitter, written from Circle's published source (the stand-in hands each message to its named recipient, as Circle's does); it has not yet relayed a real message.

| # | Attack | Answer in the router | What remains | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | A pledge arrives too late, for a void campaign, below the minimum, or for a campaign that does not exist, and the money is stuck | Whatever the campaign does not take goes to the backer's address on Arc in the same transaction | The backer holds USDC on Arc instead of on the chain they started from | `router.refused: a pledge the campaign refuses goes whole to the backer's address here` |
| R2 | A relayer sends just too little gas, so a good pledge fails and is forwarded instead | The relay reverts unless 400,000 gas remains before the pledge is tried; a whole relay that fills a target uses less than that | none | `a relayer cannot spoil a good pledge by sending too little gas` |
| R3 | Relaying a message that mints to someone else, or with made-up hook data | Refused before the message is received, so it stays relayable. The hook data is part of what the backer signed on the source chain and Circle attested; a relayer cannot change it | A backer who writes wrong hook data themselves | `a message that mints to someone else, or whose hook data is not a campaign and a backer, is refused unreceived` |
| R0 | A thief sends a message of their own, addressed to their own contract and shaped like a burn for the router. While Circle's transmitter runs the thief's contract, it receives another backer's waiting burn, and the router counts that money as the thief's | The router accepts only messages addressed to Circle's token messenger, so the body is a real burn and no stranger's code runs during a relay. Found by the audit and reproduced before the fix: the thief ended with 25 and the backer with 0 | none | `a thief cannot use a message of their own to capture someone else's pledge` |
| R4 | Someone receives the mint at Circle's transmitter directly, around the router, so no pledge is made | The app's burn names the router as the only allowed caller, which Circle's transmitter enforces | not defended for a burn made without that: its USDC would sit in the router, which has no way to pay it out. The router never hands it to another backer | `a burn that names the router as the only caller cannot be received around it`; `known limit: a mint received around the router stays in it, and is not handed to the next backer` |
| R5 | The backer's address on Arc cannot receive USDC | The relay reverts whole and nothing is received, so the same burn can be relayed once the address can receive | The backer cannot change the address in a message already burned. A message Circle gave an expiry block cannot be relayed with its old bytes after that block: Circle's messenger refuses it ("Message expired and must be re-signed") and it has to be attested again through Circle's service first. Read in Circle's source; not yet tried on the live service | `a backer who cannot receive: the relay fails whole and works once they can` |
| R6 | Relaying the same message twice | Circle's transmitter accepts a nonce once | none | `the same message cannot be relayed twice` |
| R7 | A token calling back into the router mid-relay | Refused; the outer relay completes | none with real USDC | `a token calling back into relay is refused, and the outer relay still completes` |

Not claimed: resistance to collusion or to one person using many wallets; that a sponsor will rescue; that a recipient or sponsor is honest.
